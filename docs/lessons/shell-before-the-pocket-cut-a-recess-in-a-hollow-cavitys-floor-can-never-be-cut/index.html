Shell before the pocket cut: a recess in a hollow cavity's floor can never be cut afterwards

Domain: fanuc · Evidence E1 — Maintainer reviewed
solidworkscadfeature-ordershellextrude-cutsection-view

Summary

A box part needed two features: an open-top inner cavity, plus a shallow sump (回液槽) sunk

Problem

A box part needed two features: an open-top inner cavity, plus a shallow sump (回液槽) sunk into the floor of that cavity — the shape of a machine-tool bed with a drain groove. It was modelled in SolidWorks in this order:

  1. extrude a solid block;
  2. Shell (抽壳), remove the top face, wall thickness 14;
  3. from the cavity floor, cut a slot 40 deep.

Step 3 could not be made to work at all. Cutting downward 40 broke through the floor (the floor is only 14 thick); reversing the direction and cutting upward 40 changed nothing, because the space above the cavity floor is already air. Reported verbatim:

切完发现底面被切穿（能看透）；把方向反过来朝上切，则内腔底面没有任何变化

What was tried, and why each failed: reversing the cut direction (cuts air), reducing the depth to 20 and then 10 (still through, and no longer the required 40-deep slot). Parameter changes cannot recover the feature, because the problem is the order of operations, not a number.

The SolidWorks version, the modelling mode (single body vs. multibody) and the source model file were not reported (报料者未给出，需复现确认).

Root Cause

Shell offsets the retained faces inward, so the cavity floor is no longer the part's floor. Removing the top face and shelling inward moves the bottom face from Z=0 to Z=14 (the wall thickness). Below the "cavity floor" only 14 of solid remains. From that state:

cut directionwhat the tool meetsresult
down, depth d14 mm of material, then nothingd > 14 cuts through; d < 14 does not reach 40
up, depth dair inside the cavityno change at all

The cut tool has no thickness limit of its own — an extrude-cut is unbounded along the cut direction (it ends at "through all" or at a specified depth measured from the sketch plane), so there is no "cut only 40 mm of material" option that would stop the tool at the 14-mm floor.

The transferable statement: a recess inside a cavity must be cut while the tool still has an entry path from outside the solid. After shelling, every path is blocked — entering from the bottom means crossing the whole base plate, entering from the top means crossing the whole cavity depth. The sketch plane is still on the cavity floor, but the material the tool needs to remove is on the far side of that floor, below it.

Solution

Step 1 — reverse the order: cut all sunk features while the part is solid, shell last

For the cavity + sump case, the reported working order (part 1080×520×150, wall 14):

  1. Extruded boss, 1080×520×150 — solid block.
  2. From the top face, extruded cut the cavity, depth 96. The block is still solid, so the tool enters normally.
  3. Continue from the top face, extruded cut the sump with depth 96 + 40 = 136 — one cut all the way down. The sump floor ends at Z=14, leaving 14 of solid below it.
  4. If the sump needs draft: rotate the sump's bottom face 2° about an axis with Insert → Face → Move. Do not use Draft (拔模) here — draft also slants the four side walls and changes the slot's plan dimensions.

General rule: any sunk feature inside a cavity is cut while the part is still solid; Shell comes last.

Step 2 — the equivalent alternative, if you want to keep Shell

Extrude a boss in the shape the slot should have, let the slot "grow out of" the solid, and then Shell — shell wraps the shape at wall thickness. This works, but is more work than cutting directly.

Step 3 — do not use Shell at all when the cavity is rectangular

A rectangular cavity can be made with an extruded cut instead of Shell (cut from the top face to the required depth). That avoids the inward-offset semantics entirely, and leaves the base plate as solid material to cut into later.

Verification

  • Section view (剖视图): between the sump floor and the part's bottom face there must still be solid material — 14 in the example. Seeing through the part means the cut went through; a cavity floor that is not recessed means the cut was directed upward into air.
  • Design tree: in the correct order, the cut feature's depth value (136) is greater than the cavity depth (96), not equal to the slot depth (40). A depth of 40 next to a 96-deep cavity is the fingerprint of the wrong order.
  • Numeric pass/fail on your own part: floor thickness after the cut = wall thickness (14), and sump depth measured from the cavity floor = 40. Tolerances and the exact measurement command were not reported (报料者未给出，需复现确认).

Notes

  • Domain choice is a compromise. data/domains.json has no CAD / mechanical-design value; the closest retrieval home is the machine-tool family (fanuc), because the part is a machine-tool body with a drain groove and the reporter's project is a grinding-mechanism design. A dedicated cad value would need a reviewed vocabulary change (data/domains.json), which this contribution does not make.
  • The same class of trap appears in other modelling orders: fillet before the cut that creates the edge, mirror before the feature it mirrors, pattern before the seed. The invariant is the same — a feature needs a path for its tool, and downstream features can remove that path.
  • Shell is an offset operation, not "make this a thin-walled box of the same outer size". Any dimension you read off a shelled face is the original face moved inward by the wall thickness.
